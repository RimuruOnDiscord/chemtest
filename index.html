Criss-crossed Pb₂O₄.
Reduced to PbO₂.

In ionic compounds, charges criss-cross to become subscripts and always reduce to the simplest whole-number ratio. Covalent molecules never reduce their Greek prefixes.

criss-cross: Pb⁴⁺ + O²⁻ → Pb₂O₄, ÷ 2 ratio, PbO₂ (lead(IV) oxide);

6 textbook problems
  • Fe²⁺ · Fe³⁺ Stock vs. Classical (-ous / -ic)

    Variable-charge cations need a Roman numeral in Stock naming: Fe²⁺ is iron(II) or ferrous (lower charge → -ous); Fe³⁺ is iron(III) or ferric (higher charge → -ic).

  • Mg(NO₃)₂ ✷ Parentheses on Polyatomics

    Use parentheses around a polyatomic ion only when 2 or more units are needed: Mg(NO₃)₂ needs parentheses, while NaNO₃ (1 unit) never uses them.

  • N₂O₄ · CO · P₂O₅ No mono- on 1st + Drop vowel

    In covalent naming, never put mono- on the first element (CO is carbon monoxide), and drop the final ‘a’ or ‘o’ of a prefix before oxide (tetroxide, pentoxide).

1. Binary Acids (H + Nonmetal, p. 152): Use hydro- + nonmetal root + -ic acid. Example: HF(aq) is hydrofluoric acid, HCl(aq) is hydrochloric acid, HBr(aq) is hydrobromic acid, HI(aq) is hydroiodic acid, H₂S(aq) is hydrosulfuric acid, and polyatomic exception HCN(aq) is hydrocyanic acid.

2. Oxyacids (H + Oxyanion, p. 152–153): Never use hydro-! Replace -ate with -ic acid and -ite with -ous acid. Example: HNO₃ (from nitrate) is nitric acid; HNO₂ (from nitrite) is nitrous acid; H₂SO₄ (from sulfate) is sulfuric acid; H₂SO₃ (from sulfite) is sulfurous acid; H₂CO₃ (from carbonate) is carbonic acid; HIO₃ is iodic acid; HIO₂ is iodous acid.
